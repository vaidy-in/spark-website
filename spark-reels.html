How the reel is made: the 9 hard gates and 7 teaching axes in detail

You teach once and upload the recording. Six stages run before a draft reaches WhatsApp.

1. How the video is uploaded and processed

  1. Transcribe and clean: time-coded words with Sanskrit and anatomy intact.
  2. Chapter boundaries: a maker-checker pass that never cuts mid-thought.
  3. Course map and notes: a structured map of the class.
  4. Pause judge: an acoustic and LLM judge that protects pranayama holds and demonstrations and cuts dead air.
  5. Portrait frame and cover: face reframing with blur-focus depth.
  6. Ready on WhatsApp: a watermarked draft you can approve on your phone.

2. The 9 hard gates

Four measured audio and timing gates, then five teaching gates. Fail one and the clip is retired before you see it.

  1. At most 150 seconds after dead air is removed.
  2. Dead air at most 30%.
  3. Traffic and background noise below 4/5.
  4. Room echo contrast at least 19 dB.
  5. Real teaching, not class logistics or homework.
  6. Stands alone for a stranger.
  7. One finishable idea.
  8. Right audience.
  9. Delivery floor.

3. The 7 weighted teaching axes

Passing clips score 0 to 100. Recommended is 70 or above. Okay is 50 to 69. Under 50 is retired.

  1. Interest match, weight 3.
  2. One claim, weight 3.
  3. Delivery, weight 3.
  4. Mastery signal, weight 2.
  5. Complete arc, weight 2.
  6. Said not performed, weight 2.
  7. Cold standalone, weight 1.

A peer respect test, a slide-opening check, and an other-speaker check sit beside the axes. Deductions cover slides in the opening 5 seconds, other-speaker share, dead air, and length. Those scores are what separate Recommended (70+) from Okay (50 to 69) and Retired (under 50).

How the cover and 3-second grid work

Reel + cover adds the grid cover. Reel alone does not include a cover or a caption.

  • Titles stay short enough to scan in the grid.
  • Templates and colour palettes rotate so the page stays consistent week to week.
  • Blur-focus depth keeps the subject clear and the background quiet.
How the caption, Carousels, and Stories work

Only Reel + cover + caption includes the caption, Carousels, and Stories. The reel and the cover do not.

  • The notes stay in the teacher's voice.
  • Sanskrit and anatomical terms stay as taught.
  • The caption is written for people who care about the subject and do not follow you yet.
  • Carousels and Stories are cut from the same lecture, for learners who read and swipe.

    What kind of class recordings produce the best reels, and how should we record?

    Upload lecture-rich classes (philosophy, anatomy, pranayama theory, guided inquiry, Q&A) where 10 to 15 minutes of teaching yields about 1 strong reel (4 to 6 reels per hour). Silent end-to-end asana or pranayama count-only practice does not contain standalone spoken lessons and burns your processing hours.

    Three simple recording habits that raise reel quality immediately: (1) Record Zoom in Speaker View rather than Gallery View, (2) pair a wireless lapel mic or voice amplifier to the laptop so audio stays crisp when you turn away, and (3) keep a calm, unblurred room background.
